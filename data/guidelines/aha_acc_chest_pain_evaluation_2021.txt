# 2021 AHA/ACC/ASE/CHEST/SAEM/SCCT/SCMR Guideline for the Evaluation and Diagnosis of Chest Pain
## Source: American College of Cardiology & American Heart Association Joint Committee on Clinical Practice Guidelines
## Publication Reference: Circulation. 2021;144:e368–e454
## Section 1: Initial Evaluation and Risk Triage

[Page 1]
### 1.1 Clinical Presentation and Chest Pain Characteristics
Chest pain should be considered as pain, pressure, tightness, or discomfort in the chest, neck, shoulders, jaw, or arms. 
The term "atypical" chest pain is discouraged as it is misleading; instead, pain should be described as cardiac, possibly cardiac, or noncardiac.
Features suggestive of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) include retrosternal pain radiating to the left arm, neck, or jaw, associated with diaphoresis, dyspnea, nausea, and lightheadedness. 
Features decreasing the likelihood of acute myocardial ischemia include sharp, pleuritic chest pain aggravated by respiration, strictly localized with one finger, or reproduced by chest wall palpation.

### 1.2 Diagnostic Electrocardiogram (ECG) and Chest Radiography
A 12-lead ECG must be performed and evaluated within 10 minutes of patient arrival in all patients presenting with acute chest pain.
ST-elevation indicates immediate STEMI pathway activation and urgent reperfusion.
ST-segment depression (especially horizontal or downsloping >= 0.5 mm) or deep symmetrical T-wave inversions strongly suggest high-risk acute ischemia or non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI).
Chest radiography (Chest X-Ray) is recommended for all patients with acute chest pain to assess for alternative or concomitant conditions, including:
1. Cardiomegaly and pulmonary venous congestion (suggesting congestive heart failure).
2. Pneumonia or pulmonary consolidation.
3. Pneumothorax (absence of peripheral lung markings).
4. Widened mediastinum (suggesting thoracic aortic dissection requiring urgent CTA).

### 1.3 High-Sensitivity Cardiac Troponin (hs-cTn) Biomarkers
High-sensitivity cardiac troponin (hs-cTn I or T) is the preferred biomarker for detecting myocardial injury.
Serial sampling protocols (0/1-hour, 0/2-hour, or 0/3-hour) are recommended to assess for significant dynamic delta rises.
In patients with low clinical risk scores and undetectable baseline hs-cTn levels, early discharge and outpatient management are safe.
In patients with intermediate or high clinical risk, elevated baseline troponin, or significant dynamic rise, hospitalization and inpatient cardiology consultation are indicated.

### 1.4 Clinical Risk Scoring Systems
For patients presenting with acute chest pain, validated clinical risk scores (such as HEART Score or EDACS) should be utilized.
The HEART Score evaluates:
- History (Slightly, Moderately, or Highly suspicious)
- ECG (Normal, Non-specific repolarization disturbance, or Significant ST deviation)
- Age (<45, 45-64, >=65)
- Risk Factors (Hypertension, Hypercholesterolemia, Diabetes, Smoking, Family History, Obesity)
- Troponin (<= normal limit, 1-3x normal limit, >3x normal limit)
A low HEART score (0-3) identifies patients with <2% 30-day major adverse cardiac event (MACE) risk suitable for outpatient disposition.
A score of 4-6 indicates intermediate risk requiring observation and provocative or anatomical testing (CCTA or Stress SPECT/MRI).
A score of 7-10 represents high risk requiring direct coronary angiography and aggressive anti-ischemic medical therapy.
