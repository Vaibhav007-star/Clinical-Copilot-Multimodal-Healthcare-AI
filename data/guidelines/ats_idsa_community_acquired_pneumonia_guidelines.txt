# 2019 ATS/IDSA Guideline: Diagnosis and Treatment of Community-Acquired Pneumonia
## Source: American Thoracic Society & Infectious Diseases Society of America
## Publication Reference: Am J Respir Crit Care Med. 2019;200(7):e45–e67
## Section: Diagnosis, Site-of-Care Triage, and Antimicrobial Management

[Page 1]
### 3.1 Diagnostic Confirmation with Chest Imaging
A clinical diagnosis of Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) requires:
1. Symptoms of acute lower respiratory tract infection (cough, fever, dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, sputum production, auscultatory crackles/bronchial breath sounds).
2. Radiographic confirmation: Chest radiography (CXR) showing the presence of a new or progressive pulmonary infiltrate, consolidation, or air bronchograms.
CXR is vital to differentiate pneumonia from acute bronchitis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) exacerbation, and congestive heart failure.
CT of the chest is reserved for diagnostic uncertainty, immunocompromised hosts, or suspicion of complications (empyema, cavitation, abscess, underlying malignancy).

### 3.2 Site-of-Care Decisions and Severity Scores (CURB-65 / PSI)
Clinicians should use a validated clinical prediction rule for prognosis, preferably the Pneumonia Severity Index (PSI), supplemented by CURB-65 criteria.
CURB-65 Criteria (1 point for each present):
- Confusion (new onset disorientation)
- Urea (BUN > 19 mg/dL or > 7 mmol/L)
- Respiratory rate >= 30 breaths/min
- Blood pressure (Systolic BP < 90 mm Hg or Diastolic BP <= 60 mm Hg)
- Age >= 65 years
Triage recommendations:
- Score 0 to 1: Low risk (<3% 30-day mortality); outpatient treatment is recommended.
- Score 2: Intermediate risk (approx. 9% mortality); inpatient hospital admission or closely monitored outpatient observation.
- Score 3 to 5: High risk (15-40% mortality); inpatient admission, evaluate for intensive care unit (ICU) admission if severe criteria met.

### 3.3 Empiric Antimicrobial Therapy for Outpatients
1. For outpatients without comorbidities or risk factors for antibiotic-resistant pathogens:
   - Amoxicillin 1 g orally 3 times daily (strong recommendation), OR
   - Doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily (conditional recommendation), OR
   - Macrolide (azithromycin 500 mg on day 1, then 250 mg daily; or clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily) ONLY in areas with pneumococcal macrolide resistance < 25%.
2. For outpatients with comorbidities (chronic heart failure, COPD, diabetes, renal failure, liver disease, malignancy, asplenia):
   - Combination therapy: Beta-lactam (Amoxicillin/clavulanate 875/125 mg twice daily or 2000/125 mg twice daily; or Cefuroxime 500 mg twice daily) PLUS a Macrolide (Azithromycin) or Doxycycline, OR
   - Monotherapy with a respiratory fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin 750 mg daily, or Moxifloxacin 400 mg daily).

### 3.4 Inpatient Treatment Regimens and Corticosteroids
1. Nonsevere Inpatient CAP:
   - Standard regimen: Beta-lactam (Ampicillin/sulbactam 1.5-3 g every 6h, Cefotaxime 1-2 g every 8h, or Ceftriaxone 1-2 g daily) PLUS a macrolide (Azithromycin 500 mg daily IV/oral), OR
   - Monotherapy with a respiratory fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin 750 mg daily IV/oral).
2. Corticosteroids: Routine use of systemic corticosteroids is NOT recommended in patients with nonsevere CAP. Corticosteroids are reserved for refractory septic shock or concomitant COPD/asthma exacerbation.
Duration of therapy: Minimum of 5 days, continuing until clinical stability is achieved (afebrile for 48 hours and no more than 1 sign of clinical instability).
