# 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA Guideline for the Management of Heart Failure
## Source: American College of Cardiology Foundation & American Heart Association
## Publication Reference: J Am Coll Cardiol. 2022;79(17):e263–e421
## Section: Diagnostic Workup, Staging, and Pharmacotherapy

[Page 1]
### 2.1 Universal Definition and Classification of Heart Failure
Heart failure is a complex clinical syndrome with symptoms and signs resulting from structural or functional cardiac abnormality.
Classification by Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF):
1. Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF): LVEF <= 40%.
2. Heart Failure with Mildly Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFmrEF): LVEF 41% to 49%.
3. Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF): LVEF >= 50% with evidence of elevated filling pressures.
4. Heart Failure with Improved Ejection Fraction (HFimpEF): Baseline LVEF <= 40% with a >= 10-point increase to > 40%.

### 2.2 Diagnostic Evaluation and Medical Imaging in Heart Failure
1. Laboratory evaluation:
   - Serum B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) or N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) is strongly recommended (Class 1) to support diagnosis or exclude HF. Elevated levels correlate with hemodynamic wall stress.
   - Complete blood count, serum creatinine, electrolytes, fasting glucose, lipid profile, liver function tests, and thyroid stimulating hormone.
2. Chest Radiography (CXR):
   - Recommended in initial evaluation to detect cardiomegaly (cardiothoracic ratio > 0.50), pulmonary venous congestion, interstitial/alveolar pulmonary edema (Kerley B lines), and bilateral pleural effusions.
   - Distinguishes primary pulmonary disease from cardiogenic decompensation.
3. Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE):
   - Mandatory initial imaging modality to determine LVEF, chamber sizes, ventricular wall thickness, diastolic function, and valvular abnormalities.

### 2.3 Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT) for HFrEF
In all patients with HFrEF (Stage C), the foundational 4-pillar medical therapy is recommended to reduce hospitalizations and all-cause mortality:
1. Renin-Angiotensin System Inhibition:
   - Angiotensin Receptor-Neprilysin Inhibitor (ARNI, sacubitril/valsartan) is preferred first-line over ACE inhibitors or ARBs.
   - In patients who cannot tolerate ARNI, ACE inhibitors (enalapril, lisinopril, ramipril) or ARBs (candesartan, valsartan) are indicated.
2. Evidence-Based Beta-Blockers:
   - One of three proven beta-blockers: Carvedilol, Metoprolol Succinate extended-release, or Bisoprolol.
3. Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRA):
   - Spironolactone or Eplerenone in patients with eGFR > 30 mL/min/1.73m2 and serum potassium < 5.0 mEq/L.
4. Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) Inhibitors:
   - Empagliflozin (10 mg daily) or Dapagliflozin (10 mg daily), regardless of diabetes status.
Loop diuretics (furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide) should be titrated to achieve and maintain euvolemia.

### 2.4 Prevention of Decompensation and Inpatient Escalation
Patients with resting systolic BP < 90 mm Hg, worsening renal function (creatinine rise > 0.5 mg/dL), intractable peripheral edema, or rest dyspnea require urgent hospital admission.
Evaluation of secondary precipitating causes: medication non-adherence, dietary sodium indiscretion, concurrent respiratory infection, acute myocardial ischemia, or unmanaged tachyarrhythmias (atrial fibrillation).
